Project

AnuRupa Matrimony Platform
Design, development, cloud deployment and bug-fix support of a private matchmaking platform for AnuRupa Matrimony.
Engagement: fixed price, paid in three instalments.

1Project overview

Ascent Intelligent Technologies will design, build, deploy and hand over a private matchmaking platform for AnuRupa Matrimony. It holds the profile book, lets the consultant search it on behalf of a named client, and share a curated shortlist over WhatsApp at three levels of detail — then reports which links were opened and which profiles each family shortlisted. Section 2 lists the full scope. Sections 4 to 7 set out the price, payment schedule, hosting and post go-live support.

Revision 2 — 6 October 2026. This revision supersedes the quotation issued on 2 October 2026. Section 2 has been expanded to describe work carried out since that date — the redesigned intake form, the full filter set, the slideshow, consultant follow-up notes, the Journey gallery, bulk import of an existing profile database, deletion with recovery, and admin-controlled branding (sections 2.3, 2.5, 2.10 and 2.12 to 2.15). The price is unchanged at ₹90,000; the additional scope is included at no extra cost.

2Scope of work

2.1Platform foundation
  • Web application built on Next.js 16 and TypeScript with a managed PostgreSQL database and private object storage for photographs and media.
  • Responsive interface in the AnuRupa brand design for desktop, tablet and mobile; the full admin workflow is usable from a phone.
  • Server-rendered pages with validated server actions, so no business rule depends on the browser.
  • Profile lists paged and counted at the database, so the platform stays responsive with several thousand profiles on the books.
  • Configuration held in environment settings, so credentials and endpoints change without code changes.
2.2Access & security
  • Password-protected administrator area with a signed, HTTP-only session cookie and a 12-hour session.
  • Database locked down at row level; all access is through the application's service credentials, never from the browser.
  • Photographs and gallery media held in private buckets and served only through short-lived signed URLs that expire after one hour.
  • Field-level visibility rules enforced on the server, so admin-only data cannot leak through a share link.
  • A profile withdrawn from the book disappears from links already sent, immediately.
2.3Profile intake & management
  • Intake form laid out in the same sections as the client's own forms: Personal, Education, Professional, Family, Contact, Requirements and Internal.
  • Approximately 60 fields. Personal is set out in two columns exactly as the paper form — the person on the left (name, date, time and place of birth, height, native place), their lineage on the right (surname, caste, sub-caste, gotram, nakshatram, rasi, religion, mother tongue).
  • Height entered and displayed in feet and inches throughout, including the search filters.
  • Current location chosen from India, Europe, Africa, USA or Australia, with the full list of Indian states or US states offered once the country is set.
  • Job category — Doctor, Engineer, Software Engineer, Lawyer, Business, Start-up, Govt / Civil service, Political or Others — driving the search filter, alongside the free-text job title, company, business, salary and finances.
  • Family with the father's and mother's details side by side, and two siblings each with a name, marital status, details and a potential client marker for an unmarried sibling the consultancy may take on next.
  • Contact separating the family — primary contact name, their relation and number — from the middleman's name and number, which is never shared at any level.
  • Requirements with partner expectations and an Urgent flag for profiles to be prioritised.
  • Internal with the Anurupa tag as a multiple selection (any of AM, AMP and AMO), an Anurupa Aura marker, net-worth notes and private notes — none of which are ever shared with a client.
  • Full edit of every field after creation, and an active/inactive toggle that removes a profile from search without deleting it.
2.4Photographs
  • Upload by file picker, by dragging images onto the page, or by pasting an image from the clipboard.
  • Multiple photographs per profile with reordering, cover-photo selection and deletion; controls remain visible on touch screens.
  • Swipeable photo carousel on the pages that clients see.
2.5Search & filters
  • Every filter visible at once, with no filter hidden behind a prior choice: bride/groom, year of birth, caste, job category, the Anurupa tags, and more-than / less-than ranges for age, height (in feet and inches) and finances.
  • Tick-box filters for Urgent only, Potential clients and Anurupa Aura.
  • Free-text search across name, city and profession.
  • Full-profile browse with the same filters, newest first, direct access to edit any record, and paging with a live count of everything matching.
  • Profile cards showing date of birth, height, occupation, native place and current location at a glance.
  • Search results show only name, date of birth, height, caste and requirements, with one click through to the complete record.
2.6Client-led search
  • Every search runs on behalf of a named client, chosen or created at the start of the search.
  • Profiles already sent to that client are greyed out and badged, preventing duplicate sends.
  • Profiles that client has shortlisted are badged, so interest is visible while searching.
  • Select individual results, or select all on the page, and share them as a single link.
2.7Sharing & access levels
  • Three levels of disclosure, enforced field by field on the server: Photos only, Basic details and All details.
  • Finances, the internal tags, the Aura and Urgent markers, the potential-client markers, the middleman's details and private notes are withheld at every level.
  • Expiring links with a configurable default, per-link expiry, extension, immediate revocation and permanent deletion.
  • One-tap WhatsApp sharing with the message pre-composed and addressed to the client by name; copy-link alternative.
  • Share-link register showing recipient, profiles included, access level, open count and expiry.
2.8Client tracking & shortlisting
  • Clients identified by name and phone number, with numbers normalised so "+91 98765 43210" and "9876543210" resolve to one record.
  • Heart control on every shared profile; selections recorded against the client immediately.
  • Link-open tracking recorded from the client's browser, with first-opened, last-opened and open count.
  • Client directory searchable by name or phone, showing profiles shared, profiles shortlisted and last activity.
  • Client detail page showing everything sent, everything liked, every search they ran and every link with its open status.
  • Per-client follow-up log for recording what was said on a call, kept distinct from the client's own activity so a call never makes a silent client look engaged.
2.9Public browsing with identity capture
  • Public browse page that shows no profile until the visitor provides a name and phone number, asked afresh each time browsing begins so a shared phone never records one family's choices against another's.
  • Visitors matched to the existing client book by phone, so a walk-in and a later WhatsApp recipient remain one client.
  • Browsing visitors can shortlist profiles, and the filters they apply are recorded against them.
  • Identity held in a signed, HTTP-only cookie so it cannot be altered to impersonate another client.
2.10Slideshow
  • Full-screen presentation of the current search results, in the order shown on screen, on All Profiles, on client search and on the public browse page.
  • Photograph, name, date of birth, height, occupation, native place and current location on each slide.
  • Advances on its own, with arrows, keyboard and position dots; any manual move pauses it.
  • Shortlisting directly from a slide, recorded against the client being searched for or the family browsing.
2.11Dashboard & analytics
  • Clients contacted in the last 7 days.
  • Clients with no response in 30 or more days — contacted over a month ago and silent since, as a follow-up worklist.
  • Clients who have never opened a link despite being sent profiles.
  • Clients never contacted at all — details taken and nothing ever sent — oldest first, with removal available from the list.
  • The latest follow-up conversations logged across all clients.
  • Totals for clients, shortlists and withdrawn records, with every name linking through to that client.
2.12Consultant follow-up notes & Journey gallery
  • Follow-up — a running list of working notes for each of the three consultants, with bullet points added as you type and an explicit saved / unsaved state.
  • Journey — a gallery of photographs and videos from completed matches, uploaded by picker or by dragging onto the page, with captions, download and deletion.
  • Gallery media held in private storage and served through expiring signed links, on the same terms as profile photographs.
2.13Bulk import of an existing profile database
  • Upload of a CSV export from an existing matrimony system, creating profiles automatically.
  • A check pass that reads the whole file and reports exactly what would happen — how many profiles will be created, how many updated, how many arrive inactive, which rows cannot be used and why — before anything is written.
  • Field mapping covering name, gender, birth date, time and place, height, caste and sub-caste, gotram, star and raasi, education, school, occupation and job category, employer, location, preferences and referrals.
  • Source details that have no field in the platform — marital status, visa, complexion, build, permanent address and the previous system's rejection reasons — preserved verbatim in the private notes rather than discarded.
  • Profiles rejected in the source system imported inactive: on the books, absent from every search.
  • Each profile keeps the identifier it held in the source system, so re-running the same export updates the records it created rather than duplicating them.
  • Delivered and proven against the client's live export of 2,778 profiles.
2.14Deletion, withdrawal & recovery
  • Deleting a profile or a client asks how: move to Deleted, which hides the record everywhere and keeps it recoverable, or delete permanently, which states exactly what will be destroyed before it does it.
  • A Deleted area listing withdrawn profiles and clients, each restorable in one step to exactly its previous state, with shortlists, follow-ups and history intact.
  • Removal of individual follow-up notes, shortlist entries, share links and gallery files, each behind a confirmation.
  • Deleting a client also closes the links sent to them, so a withdrawn client's links stop working.
2.15Appearance & branding control
  • A settings area where the six colours the platform is built from can be changed by the administrator.
  • Backgrounds, hover shades and readable text shades derived automatically from those colours, so a palette can never be left half-applied.
  • Live preview before saving, legibility warnings where a combination would be hard to read, and a one-click return to the supplied palette.
  • Changes apply immediately across the admin pages, the public browse page and every link already shared — with no redeployment.
2.16Cloud deployment & operations
  • Deployment to Microsoft Azure or Amazon Web Services, on the client's own cloud account, as the client prefers.
  • Application, managed PostgreSQL database and private object storage provisioned and configured, with environment configuration and secrets handled outside the codebase.
  • Custom domain and HTTPS certificate configured.
  • Database schema with versioned, re-runnable migration scripts, plus a single set-up script that builds the whole database on a new environment in one run.
  • Deployment runbook covering build, release, configuration and rollback.

How this compares.
